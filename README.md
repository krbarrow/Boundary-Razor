# Boundary Razor

The Boundary Razor is a simple framework for evaluating commitments in practice.

> Unenforced boundaries become abandoned.
> Abandoned boundaries are self-betrayal.

From this follow two immediate corollaries:

> Boundaries without action are prayers.
> An unenforced policy is not a perimeter.

The razor holds for any commitment: replace *boundary* with *promise*, *principle*, or *policy*, and it still cuts. A commitment made to another is also made to oneself.

Under this framework:

- A boundary is an obligation to oneself, defining one's own actions — not controlling outcomes or others.
- Enforcement is the responsibility of the holder, measured only by the choices within their control.
- Consistent failure to uphold or enforce a boundary leads to its abandonment.
- Abandoning a boundary is self-betrayal because it breaks a commitment made to oneself.
- Intentional change is revision. Revision is declared; abandonment is discovered.

> Only make commitments you're prepared to uphold.

The Boundary Razor is intended to cut through confusion, resentment, and wishful thinking by returning focus to the only thing anyone ultimately controls: their own actions.
